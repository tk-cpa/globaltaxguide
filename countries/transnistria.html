One-sentence summary Transnistria abolished separate corporate profit tax, corporate property tax, and VAT in 2000, replacing all three with a single sector-differentiated "Tax on Income of Organizations." Personal income tax: 15% standard rate. There is no VAT-equivalent, though Moldova began unilaterally applying its own VAT and excise duties to specific categories of goods crossing into Transnistria starting August 2026.

Political and Economic Status

Transnistria is a narrow strip of land on the eastern bank of the Dniester river, internationally recognized as part of the Republic of Moldova by the United Nations and virtually every UN member state. It has functioned as a self-governing, unrecognized state since 1990, with its own currency (the Transnistrian ruble), government, and armed forces, and receives substantial support from Russia.

Corporate Tax Rate

Confirmed directly via the Transnistrian Ministry of Finance's own official website: before 2000, Transnistria had a separate corporate profit tax, a corporate property tax, and a VAT, but all three were abolished and replaced with a single unified levy, the "Tax on Income of Organizations" (Налог на доходы организаций), currently governed by Law of the PMR No. 156-3-V of 29 September 2011. Rather than one flat rate, this tax is levied at rates differentiated by economic sector/sub-sector according to a table set out in Article 7 of that law - confirmed directly via the Ministry of Finance's own tax-by-tax overview page, which states the rates are "differentiated as percentages of the tax base, formed for each type of activity corresponding to a sector or sub-sector of the republic's economy." Newly established organizations engaged in agricultural production/processing, food products, consumer goods, medical equipment, medicines, or rehabilitation aids for disabled persons qualify for reduced rates during their first three reporting years. The specific numeric rate that applies to any given business therefore depends on its declared economic activity and should be confirmed against the Article 7 table or directly with a Transnistrian tax adviser for the specific sector in question, rather than treated as a single headline figure.

Personal Tax Rate

Confirmed via a Transnistria-based legal/tax consultancy citing the specific statutory provision directly: the standard personal income tax rate under Article 15, Part 1 of the PMR Law "On Personal Income Tax" is 15% (this is also the rate against which the law's personal tax deductions may be applied, per the same source). Individual entrepreneurs operating under the patent system who earn income outside the specific activity categories covered by their patent are also taxed on that excess income at 15%. Separate reduced-rate regimes exist for self-employed individuals and patent-system entrepreneurs on their patent-covered activities.

VAT / GST Rate

Transnistria has no standalone VAT. As confirmed above, VAT was abolished as a separate tax in 2000 alongside the corporate profit and property taxes, folded into the unified Tax on Income of Organizations. This has functioned as a structural tax advantage for Transnistria-based businesses relative to Moldova's own 20% VAT. That advantage is currently being unwound from the Moldovan side: as of a law passed by the Moldovan Parliament on 30 April 2026 establishing a "Convergence Fund" and amending VAT/excise rules, and confirmed via Transnistria's own official state news agency, Moldova began applying Moldovan VAT and excise duties from 1 August 2026 to specific categories of goods entering Transnistria from Moldova - initially vehicles, perfume, jewelry, precious and semi-precious stones, natural and artificial fur, caviar and substitutes, alcohol, and cigarettes, with natural gas VAT (currently 8% in Moldova, with discussion of raising it to 20%) also affected subject to certain exceptions. This is a live, unfolding development in Moldova-Transnistria economic reintegration and should be checked for further updates before relying on it for a specific transaction.

Residency

Transnistria's Law "On Personal Income Tax" defines taxpayers as individuals who are residents of the PMR, plus non-residents earning Transnistria-source income, plus organizations using the simplified tax system with respect to income tax computed under that law - confirmed via the Transnistrian Verkhovny Sovet's (Supreme Council) own official legislative text. The specific day-count or facts-and-circumstances test distinguishing resident from non-resident individuals is set out elsewhere in the same law and should be confirmed directly against the full statutory text before being relied upon for a specific case.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules

No CFC-style attribution provision was found. Transnistria's direct tax system rests on a small number of enumerated levies (personal income tax, the unified Tax on Income of Organizations, and a limited set of local/social charges) rather than a broader international anti-avoidance framework, consistent with the absence of a CFC mechanism.

Thin Capitalization

No thin capitalization ratio or related-party interest-deduction cap was found in the sources reviewed for the Tax on Income of Organizations.

Treaty Network

Transnistria has no independent double tax treaty network, consistent with its lack of international recognition - treaties require a recognized treaty partner state on each side. Cross-border tax relations with Moldova are instead governed by unilateral Moldovan measures (such as the 2026 VAT/excise mechanism described above) and by ad hoc customs and trade arrangements rather than a bilateral tax treaty.
