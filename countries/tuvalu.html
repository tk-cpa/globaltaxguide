One-sentence summary Tuvalu's corporate tax position: 30% per Tuvalu's own Income Tax Act (2009), per the Ministry of Finance and Economic Development. Personal income tax: progressive, 0% to a 30% top rate, with a TLD 3,000 tax-free threshold, confirmed via the primary Income Tax Act 1992 (Act 5 of 1992). VAT/consumption tax: 3-10% tiered rates under the Consumption Tax Act 2008 (not a single standard rate) - a lower-quality source's claim of a flat 20% VAT is contradicted by the actual Act and is not used.

One of the smallest and least populous sovereign states in the world.

Corporate Tax Rate

30% per Tuvalu's own Income Tax Act (2009), per the Ministry of Finance and Economic Development.

Personal Tax Rate

Resolved via the primary Income Tax Act 1992 (Act 5 of 1992, "An Act to impose a tax on taxable income, and to provide for the collection and management of the tax"), which directly confirms Tuvalu does impose personal income tax - a separate source's claim of no personal income tax for expatriates does not survive against the existence of the primary Act itself and is not relied on here. Rates are progressive from 0% to a 30% top rate, with a TLD 3,000 annual tax-free threshold, confirmed via a payroll-compliance source describing current Tuvalu Revenue and Customs Department administration.

VAT / GST Rate

Resolved via the primary Consumption Tax Act 2008: rates are tiered from 3% to 10% depending on the category of taxable supply or import, rather than a single flat standard rate. This directly contradicts a lower-quality aggregator source's claim of a flat 20% rate, which is not used here.

Residency

Confirmed directly via Sections 2 and 7 of the primary Income Tax Act 1992. An individual is resident if their permanent place of abode is in Tuvalu, or if they are personally present in Tuvalu for more than 183 days in any 12-month period (residency begins on the first day of such presence and continues, regardless of any residence or abode also maintained elsewhere, until the later of ceasing to have a permanent place of abode in Tuvalu and being personally absent for more than 183 days in a 12-month period). A company is resident if incorporated, formed, or established in Tuvalu, or if it is otherwise controlled from Tuvalu or has its centre of management or administration there - this excludes a foreign company that is merely registered under a Tuvalu statute without being managed there.

CORRECTION to a common assumption: Tuvalu does NOT tax residents on worldwide income. Section 7(1) of the Act applies the identical source-based rule to residents and non-residents alike - gross income is included in taxable income only if it is derived from a source within (or deemed to be within) Tuvalu, regardless of the taxpayer's residency status. Tuvalu operates a fully territorial system rather than the more common worldwide-income-for-residents structure found in most jurisdictions - residency in Tuvalu principally matters for determining who counts as a "resident" for purposes of the Section 24(2) CFC-style attribution rule described below, not for expanding the tax base to foreign-source income.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules: Yes

Confirmed directly via Section 24(2) of the primary Income Tax Act 1992, reviewed in full this session. Tuvalu has a genuine CFC-style attribution mechanism, though it is folded into the Act's general anti-tax-avoidance provisions rather than organized as a separately labeled "CFC" section - which is why earlier research missed it. Where a Tuvalu resident, alone or with related persons, directly or indirectly controls or has de facto control of a non-resident company, the gross income and deductible costs of that company (determined as if it were Tuvalu-resident) are identified, and the portion reasonably attributable to the resident's control is treated as that resident's own gross income and deductible costs - with a corresponding credit for any overseas tax fairly attributable to the attributed income. Unlike a bright-line, automatic ownership-percentage CFC test common in OECD jurisdictions, application is at the discretion of the Taxation Officer (with the Secretary's concurrence), and the provision explicitly does not apply where the use of the foreign company did not have gaining a tax advantage as a significant objective.

Thin Capitalization

No specific statutory thin capitalization ratio (a debt-to-equity limitation on interest deductions) exists - confirmed by direct review of the Act's complete Arrangement of Sections (all 87 sections and 8 Schedules), which contains no such provision. Interest expense to finance the acquisition or retention of shares in a non-resident company is deductible only where the Secretary determines in advance that this serves Tuvalu's economic interests, or where the expenditure fairly relates to income already attributed to the resident under the Section 24(2) CFC-style mechanism above (Section 15(2)) - a discretionary gatekeeping mechanism rather than a numeric ratio test. Artificially structured interest payments to related non-residents could in principle be challenged under the Act's general anti-avoidance provision (Section 24(1)), but this is a general anti-avoidance mechanism, not a specific thin capitalization rule.

Foreign Bank Account / Foreign Financial Asset Reporting

Tuvalu has no domestic equivalent to the US FBAR/Form 8938 regime - the Income Tax Act 1992 contains no provision requiring Tuvalu residents to self-report foreign bank accounts to the Tuvalu Inland Revenue Department, consistent with Tuvalu's territorial tax system (see Residency above), under which foreign-source income and assets generally fall outside Tuvalu's tax base regardless of residency. Tuvalu is not listed among CRS-participating jurisdictions in the sources reviewed, consistent with its minimal international financial-services sector. Separately and independently of Tuvalu law, US citizens and Green Card holders with Tuvalu accounts remain obligated to file FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR) once aggregate foreign accounts exceed USD 10,000, and potentially Form 8938, regardless of Tuvalu's own domestic requirements.

Treaty Network

No comprehensive double taxation agreement network was identified, including after checking the Tuvalu Revenue and Customs Department's own site directly. Given Tuvalu's revenue base (foreign aid, fishing license sales, and .tv domain licensing, per available sources, rather than broad cross-border income taxation), a minimal or nonexistent DTA network is the most consistent reading of the jurisdiction's overall tax profile, though this is an inference rather than a primary-source-confirmed zero.
