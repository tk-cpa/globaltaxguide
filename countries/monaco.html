One-sentence summary Corporate tax: 25% (Impot sur les Benefices), but only on companies deriving more than 25% of turnover from activities outside Monaco; most local trading and holding companies are effectively outside the tax's scope. Personal income tax: No personal income tax for residents (in place since 1869), with the narrow exception of French nationals taxed by France under the 1963 Franco-Monegasque Convention. VAT/consumption tax: 20% standard, applying the French VAT system under the Monaco-France customs union (reduced rates of 10%, 5.5%, and 2.1% also apply).

Corporate Tax Rate

25% (Impot sur les Benefices), but only on companies deriving more than 25% of turnover from activities outside Monaco; most local trading and holding companies are effectively outside the tax's scope.

Personal Tax Rate

No personal income tax for residents (in place since 1869), with the narrow exception of French nationals taxed by France under the 1963 Franco-Monegasque Convention.

VAT / GST Rate

20% standard, applying the French VAT system under the Monaco-France customs union (reduced rates of 10%, 5.5%, and 2.1% also apply).

Residency

An individual is tax resident in Monaco if present 183 days or more per year. Establishing residency requires a carte de sejour and a tax residency certificate. Given Monaco has no personal income tax for non-French residents regardless of income source or amount, residency status primarily matters for the French-national exception (below) and for social security/administrative purposes rather than triggering any Monegasque personal tax liability.

French national exception: French citizens residing in Monaco generally remain subject to French income tax under the 1963 Franco-Monegasque Convention, regardless of actually living in Monaco - confirmed via multiple sources. Narrow exceptions exist (e.g., residence in Monaco established before 1957, or 5+ years' residence before October 31, 1962, or marriage to a Monegasque national, or specific dual-nationality situations settled before December 29, 1995).

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules

Monaco has no Controlled Foreign Company regime.

Thin Capitalization

No statutory thin capitalization ratio was identified in available sources.

Treaty Network

Monaco has a very limited tax treaty network. The 1963 Franco-Monegasque Convention is by far the most significant bilateral tax agreement, primarily addressing the treatment of French nationals resident in Monaco (see above) rather than functioning as a conventional double-tax-relief treaty for a broad range of counterparties. Monaco's customs union with France means French VAT and customs rules apply directly rather than through a separate treaty mechanism.
