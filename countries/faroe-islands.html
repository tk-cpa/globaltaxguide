One-sentence summary Corporate tax: 18% flat rate. Personal income tax: Progressive - national tax (from DKK 65,000/year) plus flat municipal tax (16-23% depending on municipality) plus church tax where applicable; non-residents/limited tax liability taxed at a flat 42%. VAT/consumption tax: 25% standard rate (matches Denmark's rate, though the Faroes set their own tax law independently under 1948 Home Rule).

Self-governing territory of the Kingdom of Denmark since 1948 (Home Rule), with its own tax legislation separate from Denmark; not an EU member and not part of the EU VAT area.

Corporate Tax Rate

18% flat rate.

Personal Tax Rate

Progressive - national tax (from DKK 65,000/year) plus flat municipal tax (16-23% depending on municipality) plus church tax where applicable; non-residents/limited tax liability taxed at a flat 42%.

VAT / GST Rate

Residency

Confirmed via Section 1 of the Faroese Tax Act (Løgtingslóg um landsskatt og kommunuskatt), quoted directly in an OECD residency filing: an individual establishing a home in the Faroe Islands is deemed resident; an individual without a Faroese home can still be deemed resident under specific circumstances, including serving or permanently staying aboard a ship based in the Faroe Islands. A dual resident (also tax resident elsewhere) is taxed on worldwide income by the Faroe Islands if there is no double tax agreement with the other country; if a DTA exists, it determines residency via tie-breaker rules. A company is fully taxed in the Faroe Islands if registered there or if its place of effective management (assessed on where day-to-day management actually happens) is there. Faroese residents are taxed on worldwide capital gains income regardless of source, at a flat 35% rate on non-deposit-interest capital income.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules: No

The Faroe Islands is fiscally autonomous from Denmark, administering its own tax code (the Faroese Tax Act) independently of Danish tax law - Denmark's own well-documented CFC regime does not automatically extend to the Faroe Islands and should not be assumed to apply. Multiple detailed guides to the Faroese corporate tax system - which do cover other anti-avoidance measures such as thin capitalization in specific detail - describe no CFC-style attribution mechanism reaching into foreign subsidiaries of a Faroese parent. Given the territory's small scale and its low general corporate tax rate (18%) applied broadly to worldwide income of resident companies, this is a plausible design choice rather than an oversight, but confirm the current position directly with TAKS (the Faroese Tax Authority) or Faroese tax counsel before relying on it for a specific structure.

Thin Capitalization

Confirmed: the Faroe Islands has a real thin capitalization rule preventing deduction of the portion of interest expense paid to shareholders or other controlling parties where the underlying debt exceeds a 4:1 debt-to-equity ratio - a real, specific ratio (not merely "some restriction exists").

Treaty Network

The Faroe Islands has entered into double taxation treaties with Aruba, Bermuda, the British Virgin Islands, the Cayman Islands, Denmark, Finland, Great Britain, Greenland, Guernsey, Iceland, India, the Isle of Man, Jersey, Northern Ireland, Norway, Switzerland, and Sweden, with the network continuing to expand. For dividends paid to shareholders resident in a country without a Faroese tax treaty, the domestic rate is 18%. The Faroe Islands and other Nordic countries also apply the Nordic multilateral tax convention to prevent double taxation between Nordic jurisdictions specifically.
