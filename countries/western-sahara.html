One-sentence summary Western Sahara is a disputed territory with no independent tax system: the majority under Moroccan administration follows Moroccan tax law, while the Polisario Front's Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR), recognized by a shrinking number of states and an African Union member, controls a small 'Free Zone' with no documented functioning fiscal apparatus of its own.

Political and Economic Status

Western Sahara is a former Spanish colony whose sovereignty is contested between the Kingdom of Morocco, which administers roughly 75-80% of the territory (the area west of the Moroccan Wall), and the Polisario Front's Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR), which controls the remaining 'Free Zone' along the Algerian and Mauritanian borders. The SADR has been recognized as independent by around 80 states at various points, though several have since withdrawn or suspended recognition; it remains an African Union member.

Corporate Tax Rate

Not applicable as a standalone system. In Moroccan-administered areas, Morocco's own tax code applies - see the Global Tax Guide's Morocco page for the current 35% headline corporate rate. In the SADR-controlled Free Zone, no documented, functioning independent corporate tax administration was identified.

Personal Tax Rate

Same structure as corporate tax: Moroccan personal income tax law applies in Moroccan-administered areas; no independent SADR personal tax system was identified.

VAT / GST Rate

Same structure: Moroccan VAT applies in Moroccan-administered areas (with EU-Morocco tariff preference agreements extending to goods originating from the territory); no independent SADR VAT system was identified.

Residency, CFC, Thin Cap & Treaty Network

Because Western Sahara has no independent, internationally administered tax system, residency, CFC, and treaty-network concepts do not meaningfully apply to the territory as a standalone jurisdiction.
