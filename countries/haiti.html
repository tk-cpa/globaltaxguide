One-sentence summary Haiti's corporate tax position: 30% flat standard rate. Personal income tax: Progressive, 10% to 30% (some sources cite a top band to 35%); the first approximately 20,000 Gourde of income is effectively exempt. VAT/consumption tax: 10% standard VAT (raised from 7% in 1985).

Residency

Haitian law defines residency-for-tax-purposes based on domestic criteria, but a specific statutory test was not confirmed from a primary Haitian source this session. One lower-quality comparison source describes a 183+ day presence test combined with a center-of-interest test, broadly consistent with common regional patterns, but this was not independently verified against Haiti's own tax code (the "Code General des Impots"). Resident individuals are generally understood to be taxed on worldwide income and non-residents on Haiti-source income, consistent with the regional norm, but this framing should be treated as a reasonable inference rather than a confirmed statutory rule given the limited quality of available English-language sourcing on Haiti specifically.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules: Not identified

No reliable, specific confirmation of a CFC regime (or its absence) was found in available sources this session. One lower-quality comparative source states "no major CFC overlay reported," but this is not a substitute for direct confirmation against Haitian tax law. Given the genuinely limited quality and depth of English-language sourcing available for Haiti's tax system, this is flagged as an honest gap rather than either a confirmed "Yes" or "No."

Thin Capitalization

No statutory thin capitalization ratio or interest-limitation rule was identified in available sources for Haiti this session.

Foreign Bank Account / Foreign Financial Asset Reporting

No domestic FBAR/Form 8938-equivalent requiring Haiti residents to self-report their own foreign accounts was identified. Institutional-level CRS/FATCA participation status was referenced generically by a comparison source but not independently confirmed against a primary Haitian source this session. Separately and independently of Haiti law, US citizens and Green Card holders with Haiti accounts remain obligated to file FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR) once aggregate foreign accounts exceed USD 10,000, and potentially Form 8938, regardless of Haiti's own domestic requirements - confirmed directly via a US expat tax guide specific to Haiti, which also confirms there is no Haiti-US totalization agreement, meaning self-employed US expats in Haiti may owe social security contributions in both countries.

Treaty Network

Very limited: one comparison source cites approximately 5 treaties total for Haiti (versus 60 for a comparison country), and multiple sources - including a US-expat-specific tax guide - explicitly confirm there is no US-Haiti income tax treaty, though the absence of a treaty does not prevent US taxpayers in Haiti from using the foreign tax credit and foreign earned income exclusion. A treaty-like arrangement exists between Canada and Haiti covering Canadian cooperation-project personnel and firms (tax and duty exemptions for aid-program purposes), though this is a narrower cooperation agreement rather than a comprehensive bilateral double tax treaty. Given the thinness of available sourcing, confirm Haiti's current treaty network directly with Haiti's Direction Generale des Impots (DGI) before relying on treaty relief for any transaction.
