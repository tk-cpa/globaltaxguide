One-sentence summary Corporate tax: 35%. Personal income tax: 39%. VAT/consumption tax: 19%.

Tax System

Colombia taxes residents on worldwide income and non-residents on Colombia-source income only. Colombia operates a self-assessment system, with the tax authority (DIAN) conducting post-filing review and audit.

Tax Year & Key Deadlines

The Colombian tax year is the calendar year. Individual filing deadlines are staggered across several weeks (typically August-October of the following year) based on the last digits of the taxpayer's ID number, confirmed annually by DIAN; corporate filing deadlines are similarly staggered based on the entity's NIT.

Corporate Tax Rate

Colombia's headline corporate income tax (CIT) rate is 35.

Personal Tax Rate

The headline personal income tax (PIT) rate is 39.

VAT / GST Rate

The standard VAT/GST (or equivalent consumption tax) rate is 19. Registration thresholds, zero-rated and exempt categories, and reduced rates vary by jurisdiction - see the source link below for the full detail on this jurisdiction.

Residency

An individual is a Colombian tax resident if present in Colombia (continuously or not) for 183 days or more within any 365-day period (spanning two calendar years if applicable, with residency assigned to the second year once the threshold is crossed). Colombian nationals can also be deemed resident regardless of days present if: their spouse or dependents are Colombian tax residents; 50% or more of their income, assets, or property is sourced in or managed from Colombia; they fail to provide DIAN with proof of foreign tax residency on request; or they are fiscally resident in a jurisdiction Colombia treats as a tax haven. Residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents at a flat 35% on Colombia-source income only.

Permanent Establishment

A non-Colombian entity has a Colombian permanent establishment through a fixed place of business or a dependent agent habitually concluding contracts in Colombia on the entity's behalf, following the OECD Model Treaty definition as applied under Colombian domestic law and any applicable tax treaty.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules

Colombia's ECE regime (Entidades Controladas del Exterior, Estatuto Tributario Articles 882-893, introduced by Law 1819 of 2016) applies to BOTH individuals and corporate entities. Confirmed via a direct reading of Article 883 (applying to "residentes fiscales colombianos," Colombian tax residents generally, without restriction to legal entities) and a specialist academic analysis distinguishing how each type of resident incorporates the attributed income - a Colombian legal entity (persona juridica) includes it in its own tax return with cost deductions available, while an individual (persona natural) not required to keep formal accounting records follows a separate income-realization approach under Articles 27-29. A foreign entity is an ECE if controlled by a Colombian tax resident (holding 10% or more of the entity's equity or results, directly or indirectly, per the linkage rules of Article 260-1) and treated as non-resident for Colombian tax purposes. Passive income (dividends, interest, rental income, gains on passive-income-generating assets, technical services, and similar) is attributed to the Colombian controller; where passive income represents 80% or more of the CFC's total income, its entire income, costs, and deductions are deemed passive.

Thin Capitalization

Since a 2019 reform, Colombia's thin capitalization rule applies only to direct or indirect related-party interest-bearing debt (previously it covered unrelated debt too) at a reduced 2:1 debt-to-equity ratio (down from 3:1). Exceptions apply to businesses in unproductive/pre-operational phases, factoring companies with under 50% related-party operations, special-purpose infrastructure project entities, and entities supervised by the Superintendence of Finance.

Hybrid Entity Rules

Colombia does not use an elective check-the-box classification system; entity classification generally follows the entity's actual legal characteristics. Colombia does not have a comprehensive ATAD2-style anti-hybrid regime, though its transfer pricing and CFC rules (see CFC section above, the ECE regime) address related cross-border profit-shifting concerns.

Foreign Bank Account / Foreign Financial Asset Reporting

No foreign bank account or foreign financial asset reporting regime exists requiring residents to separately disclose foreign accounts; foreign income is reported through the standard annual tax return.

Participation Exemption

Colombia does not provide a broad participation exemption for foreign dividends in the European sense; dividends from a foreign subsidiary are generally taxable, with relief from double taxation available through Colombia's foreign tax credit system and, for ECE (CFC)-attributed income specifically, mechanisms to avoid double-counting profits already taxed under the ECE regime on subsequent actual distribution.

Foreign Tax Credit

Colombia has a real foreign tax credit regime available to both individuals and companies for foreign tax paid on foreign-source income also taxed in Colombia, capped at the Colombian tax otherwise due on that income.

Treaty Network

Colombia maintains approximately 15 double tax treaties, a comparatively narrow but growing network reflecting Colombia's relatively recent (2020) OECD accession. Colombia has no comprehensive income tax treaty with the United States, though a Tax Information Exchange Agreement is in place.

Official tax authority: Direccion de Impuestos y Aduanas Nacionales (National Tax and Customs Directorate, DIAN) - dian.gov.co
