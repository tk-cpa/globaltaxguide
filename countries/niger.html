One-sentence summary Niger's corporate tax position: 30% flat rate (Impot sur les Societes). Personal income tax: Progressive, 1% to 35% monthly (Impot sur le Traitement et les Salaires). VAT/consumption tax: 19% standard rate (TVA, under WAEMU directive harmonization).

Corporate Tax Rate

30% flat rate (Impot sur les Societes).

Personal Tax Rate

Progressive, 1% to 35% monthly (Impot sur le Traitement et les Salaires).

VAT / GST Rate

19% standard rate (TVA, under WAEMU directive harmonization).

Residency

Niger's Direction Generale des Impots (DGI) administers the personal income tax (ITS) and corporate income tax (IS) systems, confirmed via a specialist tax source; the DGI remains operationally active through Niger's recent political transition. Companies must register with Niger tax authorities within 30 days of commencing business activities, confirmed via a separate specialist source. A specific residency day-count or facts-and-circumstances test was not itemized in sources reviewed this session.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules: Not identified

No Controlled Foreign Company regime was identified in available sources this session. This is a genuine gap rather than a confirmed absence.

Thin Capitalization

No statutory thin capitalization ratio was identified in available sources this session.

Treaty Network

Niger has approximately 5 active double tax agreements in force, confirmed via a specialist tax source, and the network is explicitly described as "thin by regional standards." France is the principal treaty partner. The WAEMU Multilateral Tax Convention provides additional regional cooperation as a Niger WAEMU member. The United States is explicitly confirmed as not a treaty partner. Germany, Belgium, and Morocco are flagged by the source as "probable additional partners" but not confirmed - this specific point should be independently verified via Niger's Ministry of Finance before being relied upon. Niger has not ratified the OECD's Multilateral Convention (MLI). Since 2023, Niger has been a founding member of the Alliance des Etats du Sahel alongside Mali and Burkina Faso, following a change of government - relevant context for the practical stability of treaty administration during the ongoing transition period.
