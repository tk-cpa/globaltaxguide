One-sentence summary Corporate tax: 30% flat rate (Impot sur les Societes). Personal income tax: Progressive, 1% to 35% monthly (Impot sur le Traitement et les Salaires). VAT/consumption tax: 19% standard rate (TVA, under WAEMU directive harmonization).

Tax System

Niger taxes residents on worldwide income and non-residents on Niger-source income only, per the standard French-derived Code General des Impots structure already confirmed elsewhere on this page. Niger operates a self-assessment system for corporate tax, with the domestic tax authority conducting post-filing review.

Tax Year & Key Deadlines

Niger's tax year is the calendar year.

Corporate Tax Rate

30% flat rate (Impot sur les Societes).

Personal Tax Rate

Progressive, 1% to 35% monthly (Impot sur le Traitement et les Salaires).

VAT / GST Rate

19% standard rate (TVA, under WAEMU directive harmonization).

Residency

Niger's Direction Generale des Impots (DGI) administers the personal income tax (ITS) and corporate income tax (IS) systems. Companies must register with Niger tax authorities within 30 days of commencing business activities. A specific residency day-count or facts-and-circumstances test was not itemized in sources reviewed this session.

Permanent Establishment

A non-Niger-resident entity has a Niger permanent establishment through a fixed place of business or a dependent agent habitually concluding contracts in Niger on the entity's behalf, following the OECD Model Treaty definition as applied under Niger's domestic law and any applicable tax treaty.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules

No CFC-style attribution provision was found in Niger's General Tax Code. Niger's CGI. The specific article number for Niger's own dependent-enterprises provision was not independently pulled from the primary text this pass and should be confirmed directly against the CGI (available in full at the DGI's official site) before being relied upon for a specific structure.

Thin Capitalization

No statutory thin capitalization ratio was identified in available sources this session.

Hybrid Entity Rules

Niger does not use an elective check-the-box classification system; entity classification generally follows the entity's actual legal characteristics under the French-derived legal tradition shared across the region. Niger does not have a comprehensive ATAD2-style anti-hybrid regime.

Foreign Bank Account / Foreign Financial Asset Reporting

No foreign bank account or foreign financial asset reporting regime exists requiring residents to separately disclose foreign accounts; foreign income is reported through the standard annual tax return.

Participation Exemption

Niger does not provide a broad participation exemption for foreign dividends in the European sense, consistent with the transfer-pricing-focused (rather than exemption-focused) anti-avoidance framework already confirmed elsewhere on this page; relief from double taxation is available primarily through Niger's foreign tax credit system where one exists.

Foreign Tax Credit

Niger has a foreign tax credit mechanism for foreign tax paid on foreign-source income also taxed domestically, capped at the domestic tax otherwise due on that income - confirm current specific provisions directly given limited public documentation of this area for Niger.

Treaty Network

Niger has approximately 5 active double tax agreements in force. The WAEMU Multilateral Tax Convention provides additional regional cooperation as a Niger WAEMU member. The United States is explicitly confirmed as not a treaty partner. Germany, Belgium, and Morocco are flagged by the source as "probable additional partners" but not confirmed - this specific point should be independently verified via Niger's Ministry of Finance before being relied upon. Niger has not ratified the OECD's Multilateral Convention (MLI). Since 2023, Niger has been a founding member of the Alliance des Etats du Sahel alongside Mali and Burkina Faso, following a change of government - relevant context for the practical stability of treaty administration during the ongoing transition period.
