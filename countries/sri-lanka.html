One-sentence summary Sri Lanka's corporate tax position: 30% standard rate (14% for exports and qualifying education/healthcare; 40% for gambling and liquor); part of IMF-supported fiscal reforms since 2022-2023. Personal income tax: Progressive, 6% to 36% (significantly increased from January 2023 under IMF-supported reforms). VAT/consumption tax: 18% standard VAT (raised from 15% in January 2024).

Residency

An individual is a Sri Lankan tax resident if physically present in Sri Lanka for 183 days or more within the year of assessment (April 1 - March 31); the concepts of domicile and citizenship are not directly relevant to this test. Once resident for two or more consecutive years, an individual remains deemed resident until absent from Sri Lanka for a continuous period of 12 months. A company is resident if incorporated in Sri Lanka or if its management and control are exercised there. Residents are taxed on worldwide income; non-residents only on Sri Lanka-source income.

CFC Rules

Sri Lanka has no Controlled Foreign Company regime.

Thin Capitalization

Sri Lanka's thin capitalization regime restricts deductible finance costs where debt exceeds 3 times equity for manufacturers or 4 times equity for other businesses. Interest exceeding the applicable ratio may be carried forward up to 6 years and claimed in later years subject to the same limitation.

Treaty Network

Sri Lanka has entered into double tax avoidance agreements with 44 countries. Sri Lanka's treaty network has recently been under modernization pressure: a Protocol to the India-Sri Lanka DTAA (signed December 2024) entered into force June 19, 2026, introducing a Principal Purpose Test and clarifying the treaty's preamble against treaty-shopping - Deloitte has flagged this as a signal that Sri Lanka's broader treaty network may need similar updates.
