One-sentence summary Corporate tax: 20% flat standard rate (35% for telecoms, 50% for petroleum sector production-sharing income); civil conflict has significantly disrupted formal enforcement and split governance affects administration. Personal income tax: Progressive 10% to 15% (some sources cite up to 20%) under Income Tax Law 17/2010, with an annual exemption of YER 120,000. VAT/consumption tax: 5% General Sales Tax.

Tax System

Yemen taxes residents on worldwide income and non-residents on Yemen-source income only. Yemen's tax administration capacity has been significantly affected by ongoing conflict, with parallel administrative structures operating in different parts of the country.

Tax Year & Key Deadlines

The Yemeni tax year is the calendar year.

Corporate Tax Rate

20% flat standard rate (35% for telecoms, 50% for petroleum sector production-sharing income); civil conflict has significantly disrupted formal enforcement and split governance affects administration.

Personal Tax Rate

Progressive 10% to 15% (some sources cite up to 20%) under Income Tax Law 17/2010, with an annual exemption of YER 120,000.

VAT / GST Rate

5% General Sales Tax.

Residency

An individual is tax resident in Yemen if (a) maintaining a residence in Yemen, or (b) physically present 183 days or more. Residents are taxed on worldwide income. A corporation is resident if registered under Yemen's company regulations, headquartered in Yemen, has its place of business or management in Yemen, is an economic sector unit with 50% or more state ownership, or is a concession company operating in Yemen.

Permanent Establishment

A non-Yemeni entity has a Yemen permanent establishment through a fixed place of business or a dependent agent habitually concluding contracts in Yemen on the entity's behalf, assessed under Yemeni domestic law as currently administered in the relevant region.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules

Yemen's tax legislation does include anti-avoidance provisions covering transfer pricing, thin capitalization, and Controlled Foreign Company rules. Specific attribution thresholds and mechanics were not further itemized in sources reviewed this session.

Thin Capitalization

A thin capitalization rule exists as part of Yemen's broader anti-avoidance framework under Income Tax Law No. 17 of 2010.

Hybrid Entity Rules

Yemen does not use an elective check-the-box classification system. Yemen does not have a comprehensive ATAD2-style anti-hybrid regime.

Foreign Bank Account / Foreign Financial Asset Reporting

No foreign bank account or foreign financial asset reporting regime exists requiring residents to separately disclose foreign accounts; foreign income is reported through the standard annual tax return.

Participation Exemption

Yemen does not provide a broad participation exemption for foreign dividends in the sources reviewed.

Foreign Tax Credit

Yemen's foreign tax credit position is a genuinely narrow and technical area given the country's limited treaty network and administrative fragmentation; confirm current provisions directly for any specific cross-border position.

Treaty Network

Yemen has approximately 6 active double tax treaties. Yemen has not signed the OECD's Multilateral Convention (MLI). This is a genuinely narrow network, and the ongoing conflict and parallel-administration situation may affect the practical operation of some treaty relationships independent of their formal legal status.
