One-sentence summary Somaliland is a de facto independent, self-governing state in the Horn of Africa that is not recognized as sovereign by any UN member state, though it maintains its own currency, government, and a documented domestic tax administration (Revenue Act 2016) with a reported 6% flat personal tax, ~16.3% rate for foreign branches, and 5% VAT-equivalent.

Political and Economic Status

Somaliland declared independence from Somalia in 1991 and has functioned as a de facto independent state since, with its own currency (the Somaliland shilling), government, and armed forces. No UN member state formally recognizes it as sovereign, though it has received informal or partial engagement from a small number of governments. Its Ministry of Finance operates a public revenue portal (slmof.org) documenting specific domestic tax types.

Corporate Tax Rate

A capital gains tax of 10% on net gains and a rate of approximately 16.3% for branches of foreign entities are reported by secondary sources. Somaliland's own Ministry of Finance (slmof.org) documents individual tax types such as a 12.3% rental income tax under the Revenue Act 2016, but this project could not confirm a single consolidated standard corporate income tax rate directly from a primary Somaliland government source this session.

Personal Tax Rate

Personal tax is reported at a flat 6% on gross income by Somaliland Economic; this was not independently cross-verified against a primary rate table from Somaliland's Ministry of Finance this session.

VAT / GST Rate

Reported at 5% by Somaliland Economic; not independently cross-verified against a primary source this session.

Residency, CFC, Thin Cap & Treaty Network

Somaliland's isolation from international recognition means it has no meaningful double tax treaty network. Detailed residency and CFC-style rules were not identified in available sources this session.
