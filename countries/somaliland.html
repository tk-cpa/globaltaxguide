One-sentence summary Corporate tax: 16.3% (per the PKF Worldwide Tax Guide's Somaliland chapter, hosted on the official Ministry of Finance site). Personal income tax: flat 6% on gross income. VAT/consumption tax: 5% goods and services tax. A separate 10% capital gains tax and 12.3% rental income tax also apply.

Political and Economic Status

Somaliland declared independence from Somalia in 1991 and has functioned as a de facto independent state since, with its own currency (the Somaliland shilling), government, and armed forces. No UN member state formally recognizes it as sovereign, though it has received informal or partial engagement from a small number of governments. Its Ministry of Finance operates a public revenue portal (slmof.org) documenting specific domestic tax types, and the territory has its own Revenue Act (No. 72 of 2016), which created a self-assessment tax system administered by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD).

Tax System

Somaliland, a de facto independent territory that declared independence from Somalia in 1991 but is not formally recognized by any UN member state, taxes business profits at 16.3% (the same rate applied to branches of foreign entities) under the Revenue Act (No. 72 of 2016), a self-assessment system administered by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and documented on the Ministry of Finance's own public revenue portal.

Tax Year & Key Deadlines

Corporate Tax Rate

Tax on business profits is 16.3%, the same figure the guide applies to branches of foreign entities operating in Somaliland. A separate 10% capital gains tax applies to net gains. Business licensing permits cost between USD 200 and USD 600 depending on business type, in addition to the profit tax itself.

Personal Tax Rate

Personal tax is levied at a flat 6% on gross income. This is corroborated by the Somaliland Ministry of Finance's own official Tax Types portal, which separately confirms a 6% Payroll Tax (a directly matching figure for employment income) alongside a distinct 12.3% Rental Income Tax under Section 57 of the Revenue Act 2016 for individuals renting out immovable property.

VAT / GST Rate

The Goods and Services Tax (Somaliland's VAT-equivalent) is 5%.

Residency

No specific individual residency day-count or facts-and-circumstances test was located in the sources reviewed. Individual taxpayers deriving rental income from immovable property in Somaliland must file and pay annual rental income tax under Section 57 of the Revenue Act 2016 by 30 April of the following year. Non-resident payments are subject to a separate 12.5% non-resident withholding tax.

Permanent Establishment

A branch of a foreign entity operating in Somaliland is taxed at the same 16.3% business profit tax rate that applies to domestic companies; non-resident payments are subject to a separate 12.5% non-resident withholding tax, per the Somaliland Ministry of Finance's own Tax Types portal, functioning as the practical mechanism for taxing non-resident activity without a qualifying local presence.

CFC (Controlled Foreign Company) Rules

No CFC-style attribution provision appears in the Revenue Act 2016 or in the Somaliland Ministry of Finance's published tax-type summaries, which enumerate Somaliland's tax base as five defined categories (rental income tax, goods and services tax, payroll tax, non-resident withholding tax, and road tax) plus the business profit and capital gains taxes documented in the PKF guide - a closed list with no mechanism for attributing a foreign subsidiary's undistributed profits to a Somaliland parent or resident shareholder. This is consistent with Somaliland's tax system being built around a small number of straightforward, enumerated levies rather than the more elaborate international anti-avoidance architecture found in larger, internationally integrated tax systems.

Thin Capitalization

No thin capitalization ratio or related-party interest-deduction cap was found in the Revenue Act 2016 tax-type summaries or the PKF guide, consistent with the same closed-list statutory structure described above.

Hybrid Entity Rules

Somaliland classifies entities under the Revenue Act 2016 rather than offering an elective check-the-box system, and no ATAD2-style anti-hybrid mismatch regime addressing double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes has been identified, consistent with the absence of a CFC-style attribution provision described elsewhere on this page; Somaliland's tax base is built around a small number of enumerated levies rather than the more elaborate international anti-avoidance architecture found in larger, internationally integrated tax systems.

Foreign Bank Account / Foreign Financial Asset Reporting

No domestic FBAR-equivalent regime requires Somaliland residents to separately disclose foreign financial accounts, and Somaliland is not a CRS participating jurisdiction, given its status outside the OECD framework as an unrecognized de facto state. US persons remain independently subject to FinCEN Form 114 (FBAR) and potentially Form 8938 regardless of Somaliland's own rules.

Participation Exemption

No dedicated participation exemption regime for dividends or capital gains from a qualifying subsidiary was identified in the Revenue Act 2016 or the Somaliland Ministry of Finance's published tax-type summaries; confirm current treatment directly with Somaliland's Inland Revenue Department before relying on this page.

Foreign Tax Credit

A dedicated general unilateral foreign tax credit mechanism was not confirmed from a primary source this session. Somaliland has no double tax treaty network at all, since treaties require a recognized treaty partner state on each side, a threshold Somaliland's status does not meet in the eyes of the international community regardless of its functioning de facto government and tax administration.

Treaty Network

Somaliland has no double tax treaty network. Treaties require a recognized treaty partner state on each side, a threshold Somaliland's status does not meet in the eyes of the international community, regardless of its functioning de facto government and tax administration.
